Attic Water Damage: What Berkeley Homeowners Should Know

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. For Berkeley homeowners, our crew examines the attic, identifies where the water comes from, and dries the space.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

What It Looks Like

  • Water droplets or rust on the tips of nails that poke through the roof sheathing
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Stains or drips where a light fixture or exhaust fan meets the ceiling
  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • A patch of insulation that is flatter, more matted, or darker than the rest

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

A Falling Ceiling

Soaked insulation carries a great deal of weight. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

Lost Insulation Value

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

After months

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
